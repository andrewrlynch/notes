Adjustment of breast cancer expression data for a [[concepts/cell proliferation|cell proliferationproliferation]] [[concepts/metagene|metagene]] (meta-PCNA), derived from genes most correlated with the [[concepts/proliferation marker|proliferation marker]] PCNA, almost entirely abrogated the [[concepts/outcome association|outcome association]] of both published and random gene signatures. Additionally, [[concepts/hazard ratio|hazard ratio]] of outcome association strongly correlated with meta-PCNA expression (R2 = 0.9), and more than 50% of the breast cancer [[concepts/transcriptome|transcriptome]] was correlated with meta-PCNA. [@venet_most_2011]

## Synthesis
Multiple studies establish that proliferation acts as a pervasive [[concepts/confounding|confounding]] factor in the [[concepts/statistical association|statistical association]] between [[concepts/gene expression|gene expression]] signatures and clinical outcomes, particularly in [[concepts/cancer|cancer]] contexts such as breast cancer and glioblastoma. The mechanistic basis for this confounding extends beyond classical cell cycle gene expression, as evidenced by findings that removing annotated [[concepts/cell cycle genes|cell cycle genes]] from signatures fails to eliminate the proliferation-related [[concepts/confounding effect|confounding effect]] on outcome associations measured by hazard ratios and other statistical metrics. This suggests that proliferation programs influence broader [[concepts/transcriptome|transcriptome]] patterns that pervade many gene signatures, including those derived from random gene sets, calling into question whether observed outcome associations genuinely reflect the biological relevance of the specific mechanisms those signatures purportedly represent. The relationship between single-cell heterogeneity in proliferation programs and bulk-tissue signature performance remains unresolved, as does the question of which analytical approaches can adequately control for proliferation's confounding influence when evaluating the mechanistic significance of gene expression markers.
