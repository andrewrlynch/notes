Cells and mice with reduced levels of [[concepts/CENP-E|CENP-E]], a [[concepts/mitosis|mitosis]]-specific [[concepts/centromere|centromere]]-linked [[concepts/motor protein|motor protein]], develop [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploidaneuploidy]] and [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]] both [[concepts/in vitro|in vitro]] and [[concepts/in vivo|in vivo]]. This demonstrates that decreased CENP-E function is sufficient to drive the generation of abnormal chromosome numbers and associated genomic instability. [@weaver_aneuploidy_2007]

## Synthesis
[[concepts/CENP-E|CENP-E]] reduction, which affects a [[concepts/mitosis|mitosis]]-specific [[concepts/motor protein|motor protein]] involved in [[concepts/kinetochore|kinetochore]]-[[concepts/microtubule|microtubule]] attachment and chromosome segregation, generates both aneuploidy and [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]] through a mechanism linked to elevated [[concepts/merotely|merotely]]—the improper attachment of microtubules to chromosomes during mitosis. This establishes a mechanistic pathway whereby disruption of CENP-E function increases [[concepts/chromosome missegregation|chromosome missegregation]] rates during consecutive cell divisions, driving previously stable near-[[concepts/diploid|diploid]] cells into a chromosomally unstable state characterized by ongoing changes in [[concepts/chromosome number|chromosome number]]. The resulting [[concepts/Chromosomal instability promotes immune cell infiltration into tumors|chromosomal instability promotes immune cell infiltration into tumors]], representing a [[concepts/fitness|fitness]] cost that [[concepts/aneuploid cells|aneuploid cells]] must overcome through adaptive mechanisms such as [[concepts/stat1 inactivation|Stat1 inactivation]] combined with [[concepts/myc activation|Myc activation]] to evade [[concepts/immune surveillance|immune surveillance]]. However, it remains contested whether chromosome missegregation alone is sufficient for [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] cell propagation, as missegregation compromises proliferation in diploid cells, and the precise contribution of CENP-E reduction relative to other non-classic mitotic defects in sustaining chromosomal instability across diverse [[concepts/tumor|tumor]] contexts remains incompletely resolved.
