[[concepts/aneuploid|Aneuploid]] yeast strains show increased [[concepts/sensitivity|sensitivity]] to conditions that interfere with [[concepts/protein production|protein productionprotein synthesis]] and [[concepts/protein folding|protein folding]]. This heightened sensitivity appears across multiple aneuploid strains carrying different extra chromosomes, indicating it is a general feature of [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploidaneuploidy]] rather than dependent on which specific genes are duplicated. [@torres_effects_2007]

## Synthesis
Across multiple [[concepts/aneuploid yeast|aneuploid yeast]] strains carrying different extra chromosomes, a consistent vulnerability to protein synthesis inhibitors emerges, establishing that this [[concepts/sensitivity|sensitivity]] is a general consequence of chromosome imbalance rather than specific [[concepts/gene dosage|gene dosage]] effects. The mechanistic basis for this heightened sensitivity appears to stem from proteotoxic stress caused by stoichiometric imbalances in cellular [[concepts/protein composition|protein composition]], as the additional [[concepts/protein production|protein production]] from extra chromosome genes disrupts the normal ratios of protein complexes and cellular machinery. This same imbalanced protein composition is proposed to drive other shared [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] phenotypes including [[concepts/cell cycle defects|cell cycle defects]], [[concepts/proliferative disadvantage|proliferative disadvantage]], and increased [[concepts/glucose uptake|glucose uptake]], all of which manifest regardless of which chromosome is duplicated. While the role of protein stoichiometry and proteotoxic burden is well-supported across these observations, the precise molecular mechanisms linking imbalanced protein composition to each specific [[concepts/phenotype|phenotype]], including the sensitivity to translation interference, remain incompletely resolved.
