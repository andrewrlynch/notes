The [[concepts/phenotype|phenotypephenotypes]] observed in [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] yeast strains—including [[concepts/cell cycle defects|cell cycle defects]], increased [[concepts/glucose uptake|glucose uptake]], and [[concepts/sensitivity|sensitivity]] to [[concepts/protein production|protein productionprotein synthesis]] interference—are caused by the additional [[concepts/protein production|protein production]] from extra chromosome genes and the resulting imbalances in cellular [[concepts/protein composition|protein composition]]. These phenotypes were only observed in strains carrying additional yeast genes, directly linking the phenotypic consequences to increased [[concepts/gene dosage|gene dosage]] rather than other aspects of [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploidaneuploidy]]. ## Synthesis
Research across multiple [[concepts/aneuploid yeast|aneuploid yeast]] systems has established that extra chromosomes lead to approximately twofold increases in the abundance of proteins encoded by genes on those [[concepts/duplicated chromosomes|duplicated chromosomes]], creating an imbalanced [[concepts/protein composition|protein composition]] that mechanistically underlies the shared phenotypic consequences of aneuploidy. This stoichiometric protein imbalance drives common [[concepts/fitness|fitness]]-related traits observed across different [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] strains regardless of which specific chromosome is gained, including impaired [[concepts/cell cycle progression|cell cycle progression]], proliferative disadvantages, and increased [[concepts/glucose uptake|glucose uptake]]. The heightened [[concepts/sensitivity|sensitivity]] of [[concepts/aneuploid cells|aneuploid cells]] to conditions that interfere with protein synthesis and [[concepts/protein folding|protein folding]] further supports the model that proteotoxic stress from imbalanced protein stoichiometry represents a general mechanism by which [[concepts/gene dosage|gene dosage]] changes produce aneuploid phenotypes. While the general principle of protein imbalance causing aneuploid phenotypes appears well-supported, the specific molecular pathways through which stoichiometric disruption manifests as particular cellular defects, and whether context-dependent effects of specific duplicated genes can override or modulate these general consequences, remain areas requiring further investigation.
