[[concepts/phylogenetic tree|Phylogenetic tree]] shape analysis can be used to identify temporal associations between changes in speciation and [[concepts/extinction rates|extinction rates]] and specific ecological or biogeographical events or conditions. By correlating patterns in tree structure with known environmental or geographic changes, researchers can test hypotheses about the ecological drivers of macroevolutionary dynamics. [@mooers_inferring_1997]

## Synthesis
[[concepts/phylogenetic tree|Phylogenetic tree]] shape, characterized by patterns of [[concepts/tree balance|tree balance]] and branch length distributions, provides a quantitative framework for linking temporal variation in speciation and [[concepts/extinction rates|extinction rates]] to their underlying ecological and biogeographical drivers. The mechanistic connection operates through the way that ecological factors—such as interactions between organisms and their environment—and [[concepts/biogeographical causes|biogeographical causes]]—such as geographic distributions and spatial patterns of biodiversity—influence the per-[[concepts/lineage|lineage]] probabilities of speciation and extinction, which in turn leave distinctive signatures in the geometric structure of phylogenetic trees connecting [[concepts/extant taxa|extant taxa]]. This analytical approach enables researchers to detect major [[concepts/macroevolutionary phenomena|macroevolutionary phenomena]] like [[concepts/mass extinctions|mass extinctions]] and [[concepts/adaptive radiations|adaptive radiations]] by examining how rate changes manifest in branching patterns. However, the specific methods for distinguishing among alternative ecological and biogeographical explanations for observed rate variation, and the degree to which different causal factors can be uniquely identified from [[concepts/tree shape|tree shape]] alone, remain areas of active methodological development.
