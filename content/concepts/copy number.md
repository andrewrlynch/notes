## Definition
The number of copies of a specific DNA sequence or chromosome present in a cell.

## Synthesis
[[concepts/copy number|Copy number]], defined as the number of copies of a specific DNA [[concepts/sequence|sequence]] or chromosome present in a cell, serves as both a source of [[concepts/genetic variation|genetic variation]] and a determinant of cellular [[concepts/phenotype|phenotype]] through direct effects on [[concepts/gene dosage|gene dosage]] and [[concepts/protein abundance|protein abundance]]. Across multiple experimental systems, a clear mechanistic relationship has been established: changes in chromosomal copy number lead to proportional changes in [[concepts/gene expression|gene expression]] and protein abundance, with proteins encoded on [[concepts/duplicated chromosomes|duplicated chromosomes]] increasing approximately twofold in [[concepts/aneuploid yeast|aneuploid yeast]] strains, thereby demonstrating high stoichiometric fidelity between genomic content and [[concepts/proteome|proteome]] composition. Copy number variations contribute substantially to gene expression architecture, accounting for approximately 18% of detectable genetic variation, though this represents a secondary contribution compared to single nucleotide polymorphisms. However, the role of copy number alterations in [[concepts/cancer|cancer]] biology reveals unresolved complexity, as bulk sequencing approaches that measure average DNA content across millions of cells mask extensive [[concepts/intratumoral heterogeneity|intratumoral heterogeneity]] in chromosome numbers, and single-cell sequencing reveals ongoing [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]] that generates cell-to-cell karyotype variation even within [[concepts/tumors|tumors]] that display recurrent clonal copy number gains, leaving open questions about how selection pressures balance the generation of karyotypic diversity against convergence toward favorable chromosomal states.
