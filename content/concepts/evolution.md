## Definition
The process of cumulative change in the genetic composition of populations over time.

## Synthesis
[[concepts/evolution|Evolution]] proceeds through the accumulation of genetic changes in populations over time, with mechanisms and dynamics varying across biological contexts from rapidly evolving pathogens to [[concepts/cancer|cancer]] populations. Across asexual populations, a key mechanistic insight establishes that genealogical tree branching patterns directly reflect [[concepts/relative fitness|relative fitness]] differences, with tree topology encoding predictive information about which lineages will succeed in future evolution through the gradual accumulation of [[concepts/small effect mutations|small-effect mutations]]. This predictive framework has proven particularly successful for [[concepts/seasonal influenza|seasonal influenza]], where tree-based methods accurately forecasted progenitor lineages in 16 of 19 years, demonstrating that phylogenetic structure contains actionable information about evolutionary trajectories. In cancer evolution, similar principles apply as [[concepts/tumors|tumors]] develop through subclonal dynamics driven by [[concepts/driver mutations|driver mutations]], though additional complexity arises from [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]] generating immunogenic heterogeneity that simultaneously exposes cancer cells to immune recognition while selecting for evasion mechanisms, creating contested questions about how genomic chaos shapes the [[concepts/tumor|tumor]] evolutionary landscape and the role of [[concepts/cancer stem cells|cancer stem cells]] in maintaining cellular diversity across spatial and temporal dimensions.
