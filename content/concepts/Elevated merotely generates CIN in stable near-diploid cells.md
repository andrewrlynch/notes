Experimentally increasing [[concepts/chromosome missegregation|chromosome missegregation]] rates through elevated [[concepts/merotely|merotely]] during consecutive mitoses can induce [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]] ([[concepts/chromosomal instability|CIN]]) in otherwise chromosomally stable, near-[[concepts/diploid|diploid]] cells. This demonstrates that merotely-driven missegregation is sufficient to establish CIN phenotype in previously stable cell populations. [@thompson_examining_2008]

## Synthesis
These sources establish that [[concepts/merotely|merotely]], an abnormal [[concepts/kinetochore|kinetochore]]-[[concepts/microtubule|microtubule]] attachment where a single kinetochore binds microtubules from both spindle poles, serves as a mechanistic driver of [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]] (CIN) through [[concepts/chromosome missegregation|chromosome missegregation]] during [[concepts/mitosis|mitosis]]. The relationship is particularly well-characterized in cells with extra [[concepts/centrosomes|centrosomes]], where merotelic attachments accumulate during transient [[concepts/multipolar spindle intermediate|multipolar spindle intermediate]] states before [[concepts/centrosome clustering|centrosome clustering]] produces bipolar spindles, explaining elevated missegregation rates in near-[[concepts/diploid|diploid]] cells that would otherwise be chromosomally stable. While the connection between merotely and CIN is mechanistically established across multiple experimental systems including [[concepts/CENP-E|CENP-E]] reduction [[concepts/models|models]], the sources reveal that chromosome missegregation alone is insufficient for propagating [[concepts/aneuploid cells|aneuploid cells]], indicating that additional [[concepts/phenotypic changes|phenotypic changes]] beyond elevated merotely are required for sustained chromosomal instability. What remains contested is the relative contribution of merotelic attachments versus other mechanisms in driving CIN in most [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] [[concepts/tumor cells|tumor cells]], given that classic mitotic defects in [[concepts/bipolar spindle assembly|bipolar spindle assembly]] and [[concepts/spindle assembly checkpoint|spindle assembly checkpoint]] function explain CIN in only a small subset of cases.
