[[concepts/phylogenetic tree|Phylogenetic tree]] shape is defined by two key aspects: [[concepts/tree balance|tree balance]], which describes the symmetry or asymmetry of branching patterns, and the distribution of [[concepts/branch lengths|branch lengths]], which captures the timing and spacing of [[concepts/evolutionary divergences|evolutionary divergences]]. These two features together determine the overall structure and appearance of a phylogenetic tree. [@mooers_inferring_1997]

## Synthesis
[[concepts/tree shape|Tree shape]] is established across sources as being mathematically characterized by two orthogonal dimensions: [[concepts/tree balance|tree balance]], which captures the symmetry and evenness of branching patterns, and branch length distribution, which encodes the temporal duration or evolutionary distance of individual lineages. These two components mechanistically work together to reveal macroevolutionary dynamics, as balance statistics reflect how [[concepts/evolutionary divergences|evolutionary divergences]] are distributed across lineages while branch length patterns contain information about temporal variation in speciation and [[concepts/extinction rates|extinction rates]] throughout a [[concepts/clade|clade]]'s history. By analyzing these complementary aspects of tree geometry, researchers can detect characteristic signatures of major [[concepts/macroevolutionary phenomena|macroevolutionary phenomena]] such as [[concepts/mass extinctions|mass extinctions]] and [[concepts/adaptive radiations|adaptive radiations]]. While the sources agree on the fundamental dual characterization of tree shape and its capacity to quantify rate variation, the specific mechanistic links between particular patterns of balance and branch length and their precise ecological or environmental causes remain an active area of investigation requiring correlation with external biogeographic and environmental data.
