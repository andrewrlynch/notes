## Synthesis
It is well established across multiple model systems that aneuploidy—the presence of an abnormal [[concepts/chromosome number|chromosome number]]—consistently reduces [[concepts/fitness|fitness]] at both the organismal and cellular levels, regardless of which specific chromosome is gained or lost. This general fitness cost operates through pleiotropic cellular stress mechanisms that manifest as impaired proliferation, altered [[concepts/metabolic properties|metabolic properties]], and reduced [[concepts/immortalization|immortalization]] capacity in trisomic mammalian cells, as well as proliferative disadvantages in [[concepts/aneuploid yeast|aneuploid yeast]] strains carrying different extra chromosomes. The mechanistic basis for this fitness reduction involves [[concepts/chromosomal imbalance|chromosomal imbalance]] creating fundamental physiological constraints, though the precise molecular pathways remain incompletely resolved. What is particularly notable is that [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] [[concepts/cancer|cancer]] cells must evolve specific compensatory mechanisms—such as inactivating [[concepts/stat1 signaling|Stat1 signaling]] to evade [[concepts/immune surveillance|immune surveillance]]—to overcome these inherent fitness costs, suggesting that [[concepts/aneuploidy induced stress|aneuploidy-induced stress]] and the cellular responses to it represent contested selective pressures during [[concepts/tumor evolution|tumor evolution]].
