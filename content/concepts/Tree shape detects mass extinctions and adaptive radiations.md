[[concepts/phylogenetic tree|Phylogenetic tree]] shape provides a powerful method for detecting and identifying mass extinction events and adaptive radiation episodes in evolutionary history. By analyzing patterns of [[concepts/tree balance|tree balance]] and branch length distributions, researchers can recognize the characteristic signatures that these [[concepts/macroevolutionary phenomena|macroevolutionary phenomena]] leave in the structure of phylogenetic trees. [@mooers_inferring_1997]

## Synthesis
[[concepts/phylogenetic tree|Phylogenetic tree]] shape, characterized by [[concepts/tree balance|tree balance]] and branch length distributions, serves as a quantitative record of [[concepts/macroevolutionary processes|macroevolutionary processes]] that can reveal dramatic events such as [[concepts/mass extinctions|mass extinctions]] and [[concepts/adaptive radiations|adaptive radiations]]. The mechanism underlying this detection relies on how these events alter diversification dynamics: mass extinctions create distinctive patterns by rapidly pruning lineages, while adaptive radiations generate characteristic signatures through accelerated speciation into multiple ecological niches, both of which leave detectable imprints in the symmetry of branching patterns and the temporal spacing of divergences among [[concepts/extant taxa|extant taxa]]. By analyzing variation in speciation and [[concepts/extinction rates|extinction rates]] encoded in tree structure, researchers can identify associations between rate changes and specific ecological or biogeographical events. However, the sources do not specify the degree to which different [[concepts/macroevolutionary phenomena|macroevolutionary phenomena]] produce unique versus overlapping [[concepts/tree shape|tree shape]] signatures, nor do they fully resolve how accurately these methods can distinguish between alternative historical scenarios when working solely with trees of living species.
