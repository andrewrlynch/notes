[[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploidAneuploidy]], defined as an incorrect [[concepts/chromosome number|chromosome number]], reduces the [[concepts/cell proliferation|cell proliferationproliferation]] rate of primary mammalian cells. This was demonstrated by generating trisomic cell lines carrying an extra copy of one of four mouse chromosomes, where all four lines exhibited impaired proliferation compared to euploid controls. [@williams_aneuploidy_2008]

## Synthesis
Multiple trisomic cell lines consistently demonstrate impaired proliferation regardless of which specific chromosome is amplified, establishing that [[concepts/chromosomal imbalance|chromosomal imbalance]] imposes a fundamental [[concepts/fitness|fitness]] cost at the cellular level. This proliferation defect appears mechanistically linked to altered [[concepts/metabolic properties|metabolic properties]] and reduced [[concepts/immortalization|immortalization]] capacity, suggesting that the presence of even a single extra chromosome disrupts core cellular processes required for sustained growth and division. The shared phenotypic traits across different [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] lines—despite carrying distinct extra chromosomes—point to a general mechanism of chromosomal imbalance stress rather than chromosome-specific effects. While the consistent fitness reduction is well-established, the precise molecular mechanisms connecting chromosomal imbalance to metabolic disruption and proliferation defects remain incompletely resolved, and it is unclear whether additional [[concepts/phenotypic changes|phenotypic changes]] beyond the aneuploidy itself are required for [[concepts/aneuploid cells|aneuploid cells]] to achieve sustained propagation.
