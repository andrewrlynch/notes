## Synthesis
[[concepts/aneuploid yeast|Aneuploid yeast]] cells carrying extra chromosomes exhibit increased [[concepts/glucose uptake|glucose uptake]] compared to [[concepts/euploid cells|euploid cells]], a [[concepts/phenotype|phenotype]] that appears consistently across strains with different chromosomal duplications. This metabolic alteration is driven mechanistically by the additional [[concepts/protein production|protein production]] from genes on the extra chromosomes, which creates stoichiometric imbalances in cellular [[concepts/protein composition|protein composition]] that impose proteotoxic stress on the cell. The increased glucose uptake likely represents a compensatory metabolic response to the energetic demands of managing protein imbalance, as [[concepts/aneuploid cells|aneuploid cells]] simultaneously show impaired [[concepts/cell cycle progression|cell cycle progression]] and heightened [[concepts/sensitivity|sensitivity]] to protein synthesis interference. While it is established that this glucose uptake phenotype stems from general consequences of aneuploidy rather than specific [[concepts/gene dosage|gene dosage]] effects, the precise signaling pathways linking proteotoxic stress to altered glucose metabolism remain unresolved.
