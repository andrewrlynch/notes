Defects in [[concepts/bipolar spindle assembly|bipolar spindle assembly]] and [[concepts/spindle assembly checkpoint|spindle assembly checkpoint]] function explain [[concepts/chromosomal instability|CIN]] in only a small subset of [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] [[concepts/tumor cells|tumor cells]], leaving the underlying causes of CIN in most aneuploid cells undetermined. This indicates that alternative mechanisms beyond these well-characterized mitotic defects must account for [[concepts/chromosome missegregation|chromosome missegregation]] in the majority of CIN tumor cells. [@thompson_examining_2008]

## Synthesis
Multiple lines of evidence establish that [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]] in cells with supernumerary [[concepts/centrosomes|centrosomes]] arises independently of the classic mitotic defects historically assumed to drive chromosome segregation errors, such as multipolar divisions or [[concepts/spindle assembly checkpoint|spindle assembly checkpoint]] failure. The mechanistic relationship centers on merotelic [[concepts/kinetochore|kinetochore]]-[[concepts/microtubule|microtubule]] attachments that accumulate during a transient [[concepts/multipolar spindle intermediate|multipolar spindle intermediate]] state before centrosomes cluster to enable bipolar division, with these merotelic errors persisting into [[concepts/anaphase|anaphase]] and causing elevated lagging chromosome frequencies even when cells complete ostensibly normal bipolar divisions. This pathway demonstrates that extra centrosomes promote [[concepts/chromosome missegregation|chromosome missegregation]] through a subtle attachment defect rather than through overt structural catastrophes like multipolar divisions, which are rare and typically produce inviable progeny insufficient to account for observed chromosomal instability rates. The key unresolved aspect is whether this [[concepts/merotely|merotely]]-driven chromosomal instability mechanism operates uniformly across different cellular contexts, particularly given that chromosome missegregation alone is insufficient for [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] cell propagation without additional phenotypic adaptations that remain incompletely characterized.
