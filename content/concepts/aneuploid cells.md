## Definition

## Synthesis
[[concepts/aneuploid cells|Aneuploid cells]], which deviate from the normal [[concepts/diploid|diploid]] [[concepts/chromosome number|chromosome number]], consistently exhibit reduced [[concepts/cellular fitness|cellular fitness]] across diverse experimental systems, establishing that [[concepts/chromosomal imbalance|chromosomal imbalance]] imposes a fundamental constraint on cellular physiology regardless of which specific chromosome is altered. The mechanistic basis for this [[concepts/fitness|fitness]] cost appears to involve proteotoxic stress arising from stoichiometric imbalances, as multiple [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] cell lines show impaired proliferation, altered [[concepts/metabolic properties|metabolic properties]], and heightened [[concepts/sensitivity|sensitivity]] to protein synthesis inhibitors. This universal fitness burden creates a selective pressure that aneuploid cells must overcome to proliferate, particularly in [[concepts/cancer|cancer]] contexts, where inactivation of [[concepts/stat1 signaling|Stat1 signaling]] combined with increased Myc activity has emerged as a specific adaptation that allows aneuploid [[concepts/tumors|tumors]] to evade [[concepts/immune surveillance|immune surveillance]] and persist despite [[concepts/aneuploidy induced stress|aneuploidy-induced stress]]. While the general consequences of chromosomal imbalance are well-established, questions remain about the sufficiency of chromosomal missegregation alone for aneuploid cell propagation and the precise mechanisms by which different aneuploid karyotypes converge on similar fitness-related phenotypes.
