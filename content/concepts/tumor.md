## Synthesis
[[concepts/tumors|Tumors]] are now firmly established as complex, heterogeneous ecosystems consisting of multiple genetically and phenotypically distinct subclones that vary both spatially across different [[concepts/tumor|tumor]] regions and temporally as they accumulate [[concepts/driver mutations|driver mutations]] and other [[concepts/genetic alterations|genetic alterations]] over time. This [[concepts/intratumoral heterogeneity|intratumoral heterogeneity]], particularly evident in studies of glioblastoma through single-cell RNA sequencing, reveals that individual [[concepts/tumor cells|tumor cells]] within the same mass exhibit variable expression of [[concepts/transcriptional programs|transcriptional programs]] related to proliferation, [[concepts/hypoxia|hypoxia]] response, immune regulation, and established molecular subtype classifiers, challenging the validity of population-level [[concepts/gene expression|gene expression]] signatures derived from bulk sequencing approaches that mask true cell-to-cell variability. A key mechanistic relationship emerging across studies is that [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]], while generating immunogenic signals that increase [[concepts/immune cell infiltration|immune cell infiltration]] into tumors, simultaneously creates selective pressure for specific immune evasion mechanisms, most notably the inactivation of [[concepts/stat1 signaling|Stat1 signaling]] often coupled with increased Myc activity in [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] cancers, representing a conserved evolutionary solution that enables chromosomally unstable cells to overcome [[concepts/fitness|fitness]] costs and achieve malignant transformation. What remains contested is the extent to which gene expression signatures that correlate with clinical outcomes in bulk tumor analyses reflect genuine biological programs versus artifacts of proliferation [[concepts/confounding|confounding]] and averaging across heterogeneous cell populations, with single-cell approaches increasingly questioning whether population-level [[concepts/molecular subtypes|molecular subtypes]] represent stable cellular states or simply statistical composites of diverse intratumoral populations.
