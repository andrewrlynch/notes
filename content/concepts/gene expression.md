## Definition
The process by which information encoded in a gene is used to synthesize functional gene products, typically measured as mRNA or protein levels.

## Synthesis
[[concepts/gene expression|Gene expression]], measured as mRNA or protein levels synthesized from genetic information, serves as both a direct readout of genetic architecture and a key intermediate [[concepts/phenotype|phenotype]] linking genomic variation to biological outcomes. Mechanistically, gene expression levels are shaped by multiple layers of [[concepts/genetic variation|genetic variation]], with single nucleotide polymorphisms accounting for approximately 84% of detectable heritable variation in [[concepts/transcript|transcript]] abundance while [[concepts/copy number|copy number]] variations contribute a substantial but secondary 18%, with minimal overlap between these two sources indicating they operate through distinct regulatory mechanisms. In chromosomally unstable [[concepts/cancer|cancer]] cells, gene expression exhibits a proportional scaling relationship with whole chromosome copy number, enabling computational [[concepts/inference|inference]] of large-scale genomic alterations from transcriptional data alone and revealing how genomic instability directly manifests in the [[concepts/transcriptome|transcriptome]]. However, the biological interpretation of gene expression signatures remains deeply contested, particularly in cancer contexts where most random gene signatures—even those derived from unrelated biological processes—significantly associate with clinical outcomes, a phenomenon largely explained by [[concepts/confounding|confounding]] from proliferation-related [[concepts/transcriptional programs|transcriptional programs]] that cannot be adequately controlled by simply removing canonical [[concepts/cell cycle genes|cell cycle genes]], raising fundamental questions about whether statistical associations between expression patterns and disease outcomes reliably indicate biological relevance.
