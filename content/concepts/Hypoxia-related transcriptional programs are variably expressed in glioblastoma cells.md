[[concepts/primary glioblastoma|Primary glioblastoma]] cells exhibit variable expression of [[concepts/transcriptional programs|transcriptional programs]] related to [[concepts/hypoxia|hypoxia]], indicating heterogeneous adaptive responses to low oxygen conditions within individual [[concepts/tumors|tumors]]. This variability in hypoxia-response [[concepts/gene expression|gene expression]] suggests that different cells within the same [[concepts/tumor|tumor]] experience or respond differently to hypoxic microenvironmental stress. [@patel_single-cell_2014]

## Synthesis
Single-cell RNA sequencing of primary glioblastomas has established that [[concepts/hypoxia|hypoxia]]-related [[concepts/transcriptional programs|transcriptional programs]], along with other [[concepts/oncogenic signaling|oncogenic signaling]] pathways, proliferation markers, and [[concepts/immune response|immune response]] genes, exhibit substantial variability in expression across individual [[concepts/tumor cells|tumor cells]] within the same patient. This [[concepts/intratumoral heterogeneity|intratumoral heterogeneity]] mechanistically arises because bulk sequencing approaches mask the true cell-to-cell variation by averaging signals across millions of cells, obscuring the existence of functionally distinct subpopulations that express different transcriptional programs including those responding to oxygen deprivation. The finding that established molecular classifiers and [[concepts/gene expression|gene expression]] signatures are variably expressed within [[concepts/tumors|tumors]] challenges the validity of population-level [[concepts/molecular subtypes|molecular subtypes]] and raises unresolved questions about whether these heterogeneous expression patterns represent stable cellular states or transient responses to microenvironmental factors such as local hypoxia gradients. The spatial and temporal dynamics of how hypoxia-related programs distribute across [[concepts/tumor|tumor]] subclones and whether this variability influences therapeutic response remain contested areas requiring further investigation.
