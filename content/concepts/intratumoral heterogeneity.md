## Synthesis
[[concepts/intratumoral heterogeneity|Intratumoral heterogeneity]], defined as the genetic, epigenetic, and phenotypic variation among [[concepts/cancer|cancer]] cells within a single [[concepts/tumor|tumor]], is now established as a fundamental property of malignancies, particularly glioblastoma, where single-cell RNA sequencing has revealed extensive cell-to-cell variability in [[concepts/gene expression|gene expression]] patterns, [[concepts/oncogenic signaling|oncogenic signaling]] programs, and molecular subtype classifiers. Mechanistically, this heterogeneity arises through subclonal [[concepts/evolution|evolution]] as cells accumulate distinct [[concepts/genetic alterations|genetic alterations]] including [[concepts/driver mutations|driver mutations]] and [[concepts/copy number|copy number]] variations, with different cellular populations experiencing or responding differently to microenvironmental pressures such as [[concepts/hypoxia|hypoxia]], resulting in variable expression of adaptive [[concepts/transcriptional programs|transcriptional programs]] related to proliferation, [[concepts/immune response|immune response]], and stress responses. The clinical implications of this heterogeneity remain contested, as traditional bulk sequencing approaches mask cell-to-cell variability and generate population-level molecular signatures whose validity is increasingly questioned by single-cell analyses, though resolving subclonal architecture may improve patient stratification and [[concepts/clinical trial|clinical trial]] design in [[concepts/precision medicine|precision medicine]] approaches. A key unresolved question is whether the observed transcriptional heterogeneity represents stable cellular states or transient phenotypic plasticity, with implications for whether [[concepts/tumors|tumors]] should be targeted based on dominant clones or require multi-pronged therapeutic strategies.
