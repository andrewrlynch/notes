## Synthesis
[[concepts/cell proliferation|Cell proliferation]], consistently defined across sources as the process of cell division and growth that increases cell number, is subject to multiple regulatory influences and forms a key mechanistic link between chromosomal abnormalities and [[concepts/cellular fitness|cellular fitness]]. Aneuploidy, characterized by incorrect [[concepts/chromosome number|chromosome number]], reliably impairs proliferation in mammalian cells through mechanisms that extend beyond simple [[concepts/chromosomal imbalance|chromosomal imbalance]] to include alterations in [[concepts/metabolic properties|metabolic properties]] and reduced capacity for [[concepts/immortalization|immortalization]]. The pervasive influence of proliferation on cellular phenotypes creates significant analytical challenges, as proliferation-related [[concepts/gene expression|gene expression]] strongly confounds outcome associations in breast [[concepts/cancer|cancer]] gene signatures—a [[concepts/confounding effect|confounding effect]] that cannot be eliminated by simply removing classical [[concepts/cell cycle genes|cell cycle genes]] from analysis, suggesting that proliferation influences [[concepts/transcriptional programs|transcriptional programs]] more broadly than previously appreciated. While the relationship between [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]] and proliferation is established, it remains unresolved whether [[concepts/chromosome missegregation|chromosome missegregation]] alone can generate viable [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] cell populations, and the finding that proliferation programs vary substantially even among individual cells within a single glioblastoma [[concepts/tumor|tumor]] raises questions about how proliferative heterogeneity contributes to tumor behavior and therapeutic resistance.
