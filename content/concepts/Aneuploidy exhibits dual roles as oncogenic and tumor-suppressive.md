[[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploidAneuploidy]] and [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]] demonstrate contrasting effects depending on cellular context: while increased aneuploidy can drive [[concepts/spontaneous tumor formation|spontaneous tumor formation]] in aged animals, it simultaneously prevents [[concepts/tumorigenesis|tumorigenesis]] in chemically or genetically induced tumor [[concepts/models|models]]. This reveals that the same genomic instability [[concepts/phenotype|phenotype]] can function both as an [[concepts/oncogenic|oncogenic]] driver and as a [[concepts/tumor suppressor|tumor suppressor]] depending on the tumor initiation mechanism. ## Synthesis
Research establishes that aneuploidy plays paradoxical roles in [[concepts/cancer|cancer]] development, acting both as a [[concepts/tumor|tumor]] promoter and suppressor depending on context. Studies demonstrate that increased aneuploidy drives [[concepts/spontaneous tumor formation|spontaneous tumor formation]] in aged animals, supporting the [[concepts/oncogenic|oncogenic]] potential of [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]], yet this same instability acts as a more effective inhibitor than initiator of [[concepts/tumorigenesis|tumorigenesis]] when [[concepts/tumors|tumors]] are induced by [[concepts/chemical carcinogens|chemical carcinogens]] or [[concepts/genetic alterations|genetic alterations]]. Mechanistically, [[concepts/aneuploid cells|aneuploid cells]] that successfully transform into tumors do so by inactivating [[concepts/stat1 signaling|Stat1 signaling]] while activating Myc, a combination that suppresses the [[concepts/immune cell infiltration|immune cell infiltration]] normally triggered by chromosomal instability and allows [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] cells to evade [[concepts/immune surveillance|immune surveillance]]. The dual nature of aneuploidy appears to depend on whether cells can overcome the [[concepts/fitness|fitness]] costs imposed by [[concepts/chromosomal imbalance|chromosomal imbalance]], with the tumor-suppressive effects dominating in contexts where cells lack the adaptive mechanisms to tolerate ongoing [[concepts/chromosome missegregation|chromosome missegregation]].
