Cells with extra [[concepts/centrosomes|centrosomes]] pass through a transient [[concepts/multipolar spindle intermediate|multipolar spindle intermediate]] state in which [[concepts/merotely|merotelymerotelic kinetochore-microtubule attachment]] errors accumulate before [[concepts/centrosome clustering|centrosome clustering]] occurs. This accumulation of merotelic errors precedes the transition to bipolar [[concepts/anaphase|anaphase]] and explains the elevated frequency of [[concepts/chromosome missegregation|chromosome missegregation]]. ## Synthesis
Cells with extra [[concepts/centrosomes|centrosomes]] transiently form a multipolar spindle configuration that must be resolved into a bipolar arrangement through [[concepts/centrosome clustering|centrosome clustering]] before division can proceed. During this [[concepts/multipolar spindle intermediate|multipolar spindle intermediate]] stage, chromosomes experience attachment to microtubules emanating from multiple poles, which creates favorable conditions for the accumulation of merotelic attachments—aberrant connections where a single [[concepts/kinetochore|kinetochore]] binds microtubules from more than one spindle pole. This mechanistic link establishes how centrosome amplification drives [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]] even when cells ultimately divide in a bipolar fashion, explaining observations that extra centrosomes promote [[concepts/chromosome missegregation|chromosome missegregation]] during [[concepts/anaphase|anaphase]] without requiring completion of multipolar division. The precise dynamics of how merotelic attachments accumulate during the multipolar intermediate and whether all cells with extra centrosomes necessarily transit through such an intermediate remain areas requiring further investigation.
