Chromosome instability is a hallmark of many [[concepts/tumors|tumors]] and correlates with the presence of extra [[concepts/centrosomes|centrosomes]]. However, a direct mechanistic link between extra centrosomes and [[concepts/chromosomal instability|CIN]] had not been previously established. [@ganem_mechanism_2009]

## Synthesis
It is well-established across multiple sources that extra [[concepts/centrosomes|centrosomes]] are associated with [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]] in [[concepts/tumors|tumors]], though the mechanistic relationship is more complex than initially presumed. While extra centrosomes can promote [[concepts/chromosome missegregation|chromosome missegregation]] during cell division, multipolar divisions themselves are rare and typically produce inviable progeny, indicating that the presence of supernumerary centrosomes must drive chromosomal instability through alternative mechanisms, such as transient multipolar spindle intermediates that allow merotelic [[concepts/kinetochore|kinetochore]] attachments to accumulate before bipolar division. The relationship between chromosomal instability and [[concepts/tumor|tumor]] progression remains contested, as aneuploidy exhibits dual roles depending on context: elevated chromosomal instability drives [[concepts/spontaneous tumor formation|spontaneous tumor formation]] in aged animals but paradoxically inhibits chemically or genetically induced [[concepts/tumorigenesis|tumorigenesis]], suggesting that the [[concepts/fitness|fitness]] consequences of chromosomal instability vary with the initiating [[concepts/oncogenic|oncogenic]] event. Furthermore, [[concepts/chromosomally unstable tumors|chromosomally unstable tumors]] must overcome [[concepts/immune surveillance|immune surveillance]] triggered by their [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] state, with evidence showing that aneuploid cancers consistently inactivate [[concepts/stat1 signaling|Stat1 signaling]] while increasing Myc activity to evade immune-mediated elimination, representing a conserved evolutionary solution that enables chromosomally unstable cells to achieve [[concepts/malignancy|malignancy]].
