Single-cell whole genome sequencing (scWGS) applied to chromosome instable lymphoma samples reveals high levels of [[concepts/copy number|copy number]] heterogeneity between individual cells, indicating ongoing chromosome instability despite the presence of recurrent clonal chromosome copy number states detectable by bulk measurement. This cell-to-cell variability in chromosome numbers demonstrates that [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]] continues to generate karyotype diversity within [[concepts/tumors|tumors]]. [@bakker_single-cell_2016]

## Synthesis
Single-cell sequencing reveals that chromosomally unstable [[concepts/lymphomas|lymphomas]] exhibit convergent karyotypic [[concepts/evolution|evolution]], with recurrent and clonal gains of specific chromosomes emerging from ongoing [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]] that is counterbalanced by selective pressures favoring particular [[concepts/copy number|copy number]] states. The underlying mechanistic relationship involves chromosomal instability generating [[concepts/immune cell infiltration|immune cell infiltration]] into [[concepts/tumors|tumors]], which creates a [[concepts/fitness|fitness]] cost that [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] [[concepts/cancer|cancer]] cells overcome by specifically inactivating [[concepts/stat1 signaling|Stat1 signaling]] combined with increased Myc activity, thereby suppressing [[concepts/immune surveillance|immune surveillance]] while adapting to [[concepts/aneuploidy induced stress|aneuploidy-induced stress]]. This immune evasion mechanism is conserved between mouse and human aneuploid cancers, distinguishing [[concepts/chromosomally unstable tumors|chromosomally unstable tumors]] from those with p53 loss alone, which is common but not specifically enriched in this [[concepts/tumor|tumor]] subset. What remains contested is the broader paradox of aneuploidy's dual roles, as elevated chromosomal instability drives [[concepts/spontaneous tumor formation|spontaneous tumor formation]] in aged animals yet simultaneously inhibits chemically or genetically induced [[concepts/tumorigenesis|tumorigenesis]], suggesting that context-dependent factors determine whether karyotypic heterogeneity promotes or suppresses malignant transformation.
