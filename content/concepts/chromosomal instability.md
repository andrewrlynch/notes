## Definition
An elevated per-division rate of chromosome missegregation resulting in ongoing changes to chromosome number in daughter cells.

## Synthesis
[[concepts/chromosomal instability|Chromosomal instability]] is established across sources as an elevated rate of [[concepts/chromosome missegregation|chromosome missegregation]] during cell division that generates ongoing changes in [[concepts/chromosome number|chromosome number]], producing aneuploidy and cellular heterogeneity through mechanisms such as [[concepts/merotely|merotely]], improper [[concepts/microtubule|microtubule]]-[[concepts/kinetochore|kinetochore]] attachments that occur even in the absence of classic mitotic defects like [[concepts/spindle assembly checkpoint|spindle assembly checkpoint]] failure. A key mechanistic relationship links chromosomal instability to [[concepts/immune surveillance|immune surveillance]], where the genomic chaos generates immunogenic signals that recruit [[concepts/immune cell infiltration|immune cell infiltration]] into [[concepts/tumors|tumors]], representing a significant [[concepts/fitness|fitness]] cost that unstable cells must overcome through active immune evasion strategies, particularly via [[concepts/stat1 signaling|Stat1 signaling]] inactivation combined with [[concepts/myc activation|Myc activation]]. The most contested aspect of chromosomal instability concerns its dual role in [[concepts/cancer|cancer]], where it demonstrates paradoxical [[concepts/tumor|tumor]]-suppressive and [[concepts/oncogenic|oncogenic]] effects depending on context: elevated aneuploidy promotes [[concepts/spontaneous tumor formation|spontaneous tumor formation]] in aged animals but simultaneously inhibits chemically or genetically induced [[concepts/tumorigenesis|tumorigenesis]], revealing that the same genomic instability [[concepts/phenotype|phenotype]] can either drive or prevent malignant transformation based on the cellular environment and initiating events.
