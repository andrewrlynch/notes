Proteins encoded by genes on [[concepts/duplicated chromosomes|duplicated chromosomes]] in [[concepts/aneuploid yeast|aneuploid yeast]] strains show increased abundance of approximately twofold compared to [[concepts/wild type cells|wild-type cells]]. This correlation is consistent across all 12 [[concepts/disomic strains|disomic strains]] examined, with protein levels increasing proportionally to [[concepts/gene dosage|gene dosage]] on the duplicated chromosomes. [@dephoure_quantitative_2014]

## Synthesis
Studies using quantitative proteomics approaches such as [[concepts/silac|SILAC]] and liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry have established that aneuploidy causes a proportional increase in [[concepts/protein abundance|protein abundance]] from [[concepts/duplicated chromosomes|duplicated chromosomes]], with proteins encoded on extra chromosomes showing approximately twofold elevation compared to those on normal chromosomes. This direct quantitative relationship between [[concepts/gene dosage|gene dosage]] and protein stoichiometry demonstrates high fidelity tracking, where chromosomal-scale genomic perturbations translate into corresponding proteomic changes that drive the cellular phenotypes observed in [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] strains, including [[concepts/cell cycle defects|cell cycle defects]] and altered [[concepts/glucose uptake|glucose uptake]]. The strength of this correlation is evident when protein abundance ratios are sorted by [[concepts/chromosomal position|chromosomal position]], revealing consistent elevation patterns for proteins from duplicated chromosomes. However, [[concepts/growth medium|growth medium]] conditions may significantly influence [[concepts/proteome|proteome]] composition in [[concepts/disomic strains|disomic strains]], suggesting that environmental factors could modulate the extent or impact of these dosage-driven protein imbalances.
