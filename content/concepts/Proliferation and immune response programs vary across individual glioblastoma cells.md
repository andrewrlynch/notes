Single-cell profiling revealed that glioblastoma cells within a single [[concepts/tumor|tumor]] express variable levels of [[concepts/transcriptional programs|transcriptional programs]] related to [[concepts/cell proliferation|cell proliferationproliferation]] and [[concepts/complement|complement]]/[[concepts/immune response|immune response]]. This heterogeneity in proliferation and immune-regulatory [[concepts/gene expression|gene expression]] across cells indicates functionally distinct populations with different growth potential and immunological properties. ## Synthesis
Single-cell RNA sequencing of [[concepts/primary glioblastoma|primary glioblastoma]] [[concepts/tumors|tumors]] has established that individual cells within the same [[concepts/tumor|tumor]] exhibit highly variable expression of [[concepts/transcriptional programs|transcriptional programs]] governing both proliferation and [[concepts/immune response|immune response]], contradicting the assumption that bulk molecular signatures reflect uniform cellular states. This [[concepts/intratumoral heterogeneity|intratumoral heterogeneity]] extends beyond proliferation to include variable expression of [[concepts/hypoxia|hypoxia]]-related programs, [[concepts/oncogenic signaling|oncogenic signaling]] pathways, and even the established subtype classifiers traditionally used to categorize glioblastomas at the population level. The mechanistic relationship between proliferation and [[concepts/outcome association|outcome association]] remains particularly problematic, as proliferation-related [[concepts/gene expression|gene expression]] confounds the interpretation of gene signatures even when classical [[concepts/cell cycle genes|cell cycle genes]] are removed, suggesting that proliferative effects permeate broader transcriptional programs including those related to immune response. What remains contested is whether the variable expression of immune response programs within individual [[concepts/tumor cells|tumor cells]] represents genuine functional heterogeneity in immune signaling or is an artifact of proliferation-related [[concepts/confounding|confounding]] that undermines the biological relevance of these population-level molecular classifications.
