The correlation between increased [[concepts/protein abundance|protein abundance]] and [[concepts/gene dosage|gene dosage]] in [[concepts/aneuploid yeast|aneuploid yeast]] becomes apparent when [[concepts/log2 ratios|log2 ratios]] of protein levels are sorted by the [[concepts/chromosomal position|chromosomal position]] of their encoding genes. Proteins encoded on [[concepts/duplicated chromosomes|duplicated chromosomes]] show consistent elevation relative to those on non-duplicated chromosomes, demonstrating a direct relationship between gene [[concepts/copy number|copy number]] and protein abundance. [@dephoure_quantitative_2014]

## Synthesis
Quantitative proteomics studies using [[concepts/silac|SILAC]] and mass spectrometry in [[concepts/aneuploid yeast|aneuploid yeast]] strains have established that [[concepts/protein abundance|protein abundance]] changes scale directly with chromosomal [[concepts/copy number|copy number]], demonstrating that proteins encoded on [[concepts/duplicated chromosomes|duplicated chromosomes]] consistently increase approximately twofold in abundance compared to [[concepts/wild type cells|wild-type cells]]. This proportional relationship between [[concepts/gene dosage|gene dosage]] and protein stoichiometry has been observed across all examined [[concepts/disomic strains|disomic strains]], with measurable [[concepts/log2 ratios|log2 ratios]] correlating with [[concepts/chromosomal position|chromosomal position]], indicating that protein levels track the physical location of genes on duplicated chromosomes. The mechanistic basis for this high-fidelity response appears to be a direct, largely uncompensated translation of increased gene copy number into proportional protein output, creating systematic imbalances in cellular [[concepts/protein composition|protein composition]]. While the correlation between chromosomal position and protein abundance is well-established, the related concept notes do not address whether any compensatory mechanisms or regulatory feedbacks might modulate this response for specific protein classes or functional categories.
