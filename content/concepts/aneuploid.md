## Definition
Having an abnormal number of chromosomes that deviates from the typical diploid complement.

A condition characterized by an abnormal number of chromosomes, either more or fewer than the normal diploid number.

## Synthesis
Aneuploidy, consistently defined across sources as an abnormal [[concepts/chromosome number|chromosome number]] that deviates from the normal [[concepts/diploid|diploid]] or [[concepts/euploid|euploid]] state, imposes fundamental cellular costs through multiple mechanistic pathways. The condition creates a proteotoxic burden because proteins encoded by genes on [[concepts/duplicated chromosomes|duplicated chromosomes]] increase approximately twofold in abundance, disrupting stoichiometric balance in protein complexes and triggering widespread metabolic alterations, [[concepts/cell cycle defects|cell cycle defects]], proliferative disadvantages, and heightened [[concepts/sensitivity|sensitivity]] to protein synthesis interference—effects remarkably consistent regardless of which specific chromosome is gained. However, the relationship between aneuploidy and [[concepts/cancer|cancer]] remains contested, with evidence showing it both drives [[concepts/spontaneous tumor formation|spontaneous tumor formation]] in aged animals and simultaneously inhibits chemically or genetically induced [[concepts/tumorigenesis|tumorigenesis]], revealing context-dependent [[concepts/oncogenic|oncogenic]] and [[concepts/tumor|tumor]]-suppressive roles. Recent work has begun to resolve this paradox by demonstrating that [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] cancer cells must actively suppress [[concepts/immune surveillance|immune surveillance]] through specific mechanisms such as [[concepts/stat1 inactivation|Stat1 inactivation]] combined with [[concepts/myc activation|Myc activation]], suggesting that the [[concepts/fitness|fitness]] costs of aneuploidy create immunogenic stress that cells must overcome to achieve [[concepts/malignancy|malignancy]], though the precise mechanisms linking [[concepts/chromosomal instability|chromosomal instability]] to ongoing aneuploidy generation in most [[concepts/tumor cells|tumor cells]] remain incompletely understood.
