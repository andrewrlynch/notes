[[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploidAneuploidy]] increases genomic instability and [[concepts/chromosome loss|chromosome loss]] rates, requiring [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] strains to be grown in selective medium that maintains the duplicated chromosome. Different growth media (synthetic medium for [[concepts/silac|SILAC]] versus rich YEPD medium for TMT analysis) may significantly influence the [[concepts/proteome|proteome]] composition of [[concepts/disomic strains|disomic strains]] and their responses to aneuploidy. ## Synthesis
Studies using quantitative proteomics techniques such as [[concepts/silac|SILAC]] and tandem mass tag mass spectrometry on [[concepts/aneuploid yeast|aneuploid yeast]] strains have established that [[concepts/proteome|proteome]] composition is consistently altered by changes in [[concepts/chromosome number|chromosome number]], with proteins encoded on [[concepts/duplicated chromosomes|duplicated chromosomes]] showing approximately twofold increases in abundance that track [[concepts/gene dosage|gene dosage]] with high fidelity across all examined [[concepts/disomic strains|disomic strains]]. However, the specific [[concepts/growth medium|growth medium]] conditions under which these [[concepts/aneuploid|aneuploid]] strains are cultivated have been shown to affect their proteome composition, suggesting that environmental factors modulate the proteomic consequences of chromosomal imbalances. The mechanistic basis for how growth medium conditions interact with aneuploidy to shape the proteome remains an area requiring further investigation, as it is unclear whether nutritional differences trigger compensatory responses, alter protein degradation pathways, or influence the cellular capacity to buffer the stoichiometric imbalances created by chromosome gain or loss.
